ChatterLab · University of Chicago · Jun 2026 → present

Capturing multiparty interactions across the developmental trajectory

with Dr. Marisa Casillas and Subin Kim (2nd-year PhD)

How does a child break into a conversation? I work on detecting the moment a child enters multiparty talk in naturalistic daylong recordings. The diagnostic is semantic alignment. When speakers are engaged in the same interaction, their language converges in meaning, so alignment that rises above baseline can help us identify multiparty talk. I measure it with BERTScore, using transformer embeddings to compare the meaning of neighbouring turns, within the ALIGN 2.0 framework.

Much of my work is the pipeline that makes this analysis possible. I carry ELAN-annotated recordings into the format the tools expect, preserve the messy realities of child speech such as sung and wordless vocalisations, and run alignment measures over the result. I presented this research at the ICIS Founding Generation Symposium in September 2026, on identifying multiparty conversation through both temporal and alignment-based measures. I'm now working towards a CogSci submission.

Understanding Reading Development through Visual and Linguistic Factors

with Dr. Anastasia Stoops, Dr. Jessica Montag, and Dr. Volodymyr Kindratenko (NCSA)

Examining whether deep learning architectures, from LSTMs to transformer models like BERT, can predict the eye movements of teens and adults from the visual and linguistic features of text. I scaled the fixation corpus from 225k to over 800k fixations, training on NCSA Delta GPUs.
